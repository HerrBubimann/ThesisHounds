# Automation bias: evidence and design implications

Research checked 2026-11-28 using public primary sources only; no thesis or human
review content was sent to web search. The original ThesisHounds project and
professor feedback call for scientific assistance and human decision authority.

| Primary source | Finding and limitation | ThesisHounds design inference |
|---|---|---|
| Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos (2021), *To Trust or to Think*, DOI [10.1145/3449287](https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692) | In a 199-participant experiment, cognitive forcing reduced overreliance relative to simple explanation interfaces. More effective interventions received less favorable subjective ratings; effects varied with motivation to think. This was not a thesis-grading study. | Require a separately submitted human review before disclosure; measure usability and unequal effects rather than assuming universal benefit. |
| Bansal et al. (2021), [*Does the Whole Exceed its Parts?*](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/does-the-whole-exceed-its-parts-the-effect-of-ai-explanations-on-complementary-team-performance/) | Across three datasets, explanations did not increase complementary team performance and increased recommendation acceptance regardless of correctness. | Present original supporting/counterevidence and permit rejection; an explanation or matching label is not a quality certificate. |
| Dietvorst, Simmons & Massey (2015), *Algorithm Aversion*, DOI [10.1037/xge0000033](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000033), [author copy](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/20-Dietvorst-Simmons-Massey-2015.pdf) | Forecasting studies found reduced willingness to use algorithms after observing errors, including when algorithms outperformed humans. Transfer to scientific assessment is untested. | Low adoption is not inherently desirable. Measure correct adoption and unjustified rejection alongside adoption of false criticism. |
| EU AI Act, [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32024R1689) | For systems within its high-risk scope, human oversight addresses limitations, automation bias, interpretation, overriding and stopping. | Use explicit release, inspectable evidence, refusal/override and cancellation as design inputs. This is not a legal classification or conformity assessment. |

These are design inferences, not evidence that this implementation reduces bias in
DHBW examiners. Independent judgment is operationalized as separate inputs and
disclosure order, not a measurable inner mental state. Reviewers may already know
similar outputs, paste superficial judgments or experience anchoring after release.
No surveillance or artificial reading delay can establish genuine independence.

Evidence-first comparison explicitly labels category agreement as coarse and leaves
semantic agreement unestablished. Abstention and unresolved disagreement are valid.
Scientific responsibility, rubric applicability and final examination decisions stay
with the authorized human. Institution-specific assessment research, approved study
materials and expert reference judgments are still needed.
